# Social Engineering Scam Awareness: Fake Student Emergency & Touch 'n Go Scam

![Category](https://img.shields.io/badge/Category-Cybersecurity%20Awareness-blue)
![Threat-Type](https://img.shields.io/badge/Threat--Type-Social%20Engineering%20%2F%20Impersonation-red)

## 📌 Overview
This repository documents a real-life cross-border **Social Engineering and Financial Fraud** attempt targeting Bangladeshis and international students/residents in Malaysia. The attacker leverages emotional manipulation, stolen identification, and fake payment confirmations to steal money via mobile financial services (MFS) like **Touch 'n Go (TnG)** and **bKash**.

---

## 🎭 Scam Scenario Breakdown

1. **Initial Contact:** 
   * Attacker contacts the victim via WhatsApp from an unfamiliar Malaysian (+60) phone number.
   * Claims to be a newly arrived international student who is stranded, knows no one, and urgently needs local Malaysian Ringgit (MYR).
2. **Emotional Exploitation (Sympathy Scam):**
   * Promises that their mother in Bangladesh will send equivalent money via bKash.
   * Sends a photo of a Bangladeshi Passport (approx. 18–20 years old) as "proof of identity" to gain trust.
3. **Fake Payment Execution (SMS Spoofing / Fake Proof):**
   * Refuses bank transfers and insists on using bKash.
   * Sends a fake bKash app transaction screenshot along with a fake "Cash In / Payment Received" SMS sent from a regular private mobile number instead of the official **bKash** sender ID.
4. **Fund Extraction:**
   * Claims not to have a local bank account and requests funds transferred directly to a **Touch 'n Go eWallet** account.
5. **Abort Phase:**
   * Once confronted about the fraudulent SMS, the scammer stops responding immediately.

---

## 🛑 Cybersecurity Terminology & Attack Classification

* **Social Engineering:** Manipulating individuals into performing actions or divulging confidential information through psychological exploitation.
* **Impersonation Attack:** Pretending to be an innocent, vulnerable student in distress to lower the target's vigilance.
* **Advance-Fee / Empathy Scam:** Exploiting goodwill, kindness, and human sympathy for illicit financial gain.
* **SMS Spoofing / Fake SMS Fraud:** Using arbitrary phone numbers to simulate official system notifications from financial service providers.
* **Identity Theft / Credential Misuse:** Using someone else's stolen or leaked passport photo to establish false credibility.

## 🔍 Red Flags to Watch Out For

| Red Flag | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Unsolicited Requests from Strangers** | Never exchange currency or send money to individuals met online. |
| **Regular Mobile Number for MFS SMS** | Real bKash/Nagad SMS notifications always come from official branded sender IDs (e.g., `bKash`), **never** from standard 11-digit mobile numbers. |
| **Refusal of Bank Transfers** | Fraudulent actors prefer untraceable or fast-exit channels (like eWallets) over standard bank-to-bank wire transfers. |
| **Pipelined Urgency & Emotional Guilt** | Continuous pressure framed around "emergency", "helplessness", or "isolation". |

---

## 🛡️ Mitigation & Security Best Practices

1. **Verify Official Sender IDs:** Always check your actual account balance directly through the official app (bKash/Nagad/Bank) instead of relying on incoming SMS notifications or screenshots.
2. **Never Accept ID Scans as Proof:** A photo of a passport or NID provided over messaging apps proves nothing; it is often stolen or purchased off dark web forums/leaks.
3. **Protect Personal Data:** Avoid sharing images of your passport, national ID, or confidential credentials with unverified contacts online.
4. **Report & Block:** Block suspicious WhatsApp numbers and report fraudulent eWallet/bKash accounts to the respective platform authorities immediately.

---

> ⚠️ **Note:** Scams like this undermine social trust and make it harder for real people in distress to receive help. Spread awareness to protect your community!
